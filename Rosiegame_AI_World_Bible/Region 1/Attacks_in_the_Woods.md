# The Attacks in the Woods
**Status:** PROVISIONAL NARRATIVE — creature identity and deeper details intentionally undecided

## Overview

For generations, attacks have occurred around the great forest and the settlements that depend on it. In recent years, they have become more frequent and more dangerous. The attacks threaten the lumber settlement, its workers, equipment and supplies, and the safety of people travelling through the woods.

The creatures responsible for the attacks are not yet defined. The existing game mechanic that produces groups of hostile creatures can remain in place for now. These are not to be called goblins in the established narrative unless that is chosen later.

## The Hidden People

An old rumour tells of a people who once lived in the forest and fought on the losing side of the ancient War of the Two Crowns. The accepted account says they were wiped out long ago. Most people regard the story as myth or legend.

In truth, this people survived. They have remained in a hidden refuge deep in the forest for at least a thousand years, preserving their identity and their inherited loyalty to the defeated side. Their home is extremely difficult to find and attack: a network of caves, concealed entrances and defensible passages in terrain that outsiders struggle to navigate.

Their population is small. Their refuge protects them from conquest, but its limited space, resources and difficult terrain prevent them from growing into a large conventional power.

## The Wizards and the Creatures

The attacks may involve more than the creatures themselves. During an attack, one or several distinct human-like figures — described provisionally as wizardly men — may accompany the creatures.

They do not usually advance as far as the creatures. They appear to direct, control or herd them, then withdraw rather than remain to fight the defenders themselves. This behaviour is a possible explanation for how the attacks are coordinated, but the exact method is not yet defined.

The wizardly figures are associated with the hidden people and may be connected to the Everlasting Court or its wider network. The precise relationship remains provisional: the Court may be secretly supporting the hidden people, but the extent of its involvement has not been settled.

The creatures’ identity, origin, nature and means of control are deliberately OPEN QUESTIONS. Do not invent a definitive answer for them yet.

## What the Settlement Knows

The settlement has not necessarily recognised the pattern of the wizardly figures appearing alongside the creatures.

- Most inhabitants may believe the attacks are caused by dangerous creatures from the forest.
- Some witnesses may have noticed separate figures accompanying them, but dismissed them as travellers, strange onlookers, or people who happen to be nearby.
- Others may have glimpsed something they cannot explain, but have not connected separate incidents.
- The commander is concerned about the growing frequency and danger of the attacks, but need not understand their true cause.

These are possibilities for individual NPC knowledge, not a claim that every inhabitant has the same information. Knowledge should depend on what each person has personally witnessed or heard.

## How an Attack Can Appear in Play

If the player is present when an attack occurs, they may see a sequence such as:

1. Hostile creatures emerge from the woods and attack a target such as the lumber settlement, workers, equipment or a supply route.
2. One or several distinct figures appear with them, but remain farther back or near the edge of the action.
3. The figures seem to direct or herd the creatures rather than fight like ordinary attackers.
4. The figures withdraw, and the creatures may retreat, disperse or continue their attack according to the existing game mechanics.

The player is not automatically told what this means. They witness observable behaviour and must draw their own conclusions. If they are not present, they may only hear reports, and those reports can be incomplete or mistaken.

The current game mechanic can continue to spawn and manage groups of hostile creatures. The narrative interpretation is that these attacks may be orchestrated by the hidden people and their wizardly agents. The precise creature type and implementation are not yet decided.

## Discovery and Escalation

The truth should emerge gradually rather than being explained at the start.

Possible clues include:
- reports that attacks have increased in frequency;
- accounts of figures seen behind the creatures;
- differences between the creatures’ behaviour when those figures are present or absent;
- tracks, signs or other evidence suggesting the creatures are being directed;
- eventually, evidence of a concealed route or entrance leading deeper into the forest.

The hidden refuge should not be revealed until the player discovers credible evidence of it. Early dialogue may mention the old legend, but NPCs should generally treat it as a myth because the accepted history says the people were destroyed.

## Godwin's Rumour

Godwin, the innkeeper, can explain the local legend without knowing it is true. In substance, he might say:

> “There's an old tale about a people who lived out there, long ago. Fought on the losing side of some ancient war, or so the story goes. They were supposed to have been wiped out.”

If asked whether he thinks they are behind the attacks, he dismisses it as a story people tell when they cannot explain what is happening. He may acknowledge that the attacks have grown worse and tend to happen around the woods, without connecting this to the legend.

Godwin is repeating a rumour, not revealing secret knowledge. Keep his dialogue short and conversational.

## Established Direction and Open Questions

### Current working direction
- The attacks around the woods are becoming more frequent and dangerous.
- A hidden people, believed by most to have been destroyed, survived in a hard-to-find forest refuge.
- They remained loyal to the defeated side in the ancient war.
- Distinct wizardly figures may accompany and direct the hostile creatures, then withdraw.
- The existing creature-attack mechanic can be retained while the creatures' identity remains undecided.
- The Everlasting Court may be secretly supporting the hidden people, but the exact relationship is not final.

### Open questions — do not decide without further direction
- What are the creatures?
- Where did they come from, and what exactly are they?
- How do the wizardly figures direct or control them?
- Are the figures members of the hidden people, agents of the Everlasting Court, or a mixture?
- What does the Everlasting Court want from the hidden people?
- What did the hidden people do during the War of the Two Crowns?
- What name do the hidden people use for themselves, and what do outsiders call them?
- What evidence ultimately leads the player to the refuge?
- How much does the local commander know, and what have individual townspeople witnessed?

Treat this file as a separate narrative situation file. Do not merge it into the region file or use it to settle any of the open questions above.
