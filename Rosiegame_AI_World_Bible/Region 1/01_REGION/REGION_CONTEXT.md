# First Region — Context
**Working foundation; some details provisional**

## Position
A settled, productive region on the outer reaches of the New Empire: integrated into imperial life but distant enough for local families to retain authority and customs. Inspiration combines Roman Britain and medieval northern Britain, without copying either literally.

## Local lordship
The existing settlement is a small fortified seat of a local lord, not a city. Preserve the current lord's house, barracks, blacksmith, inn, shop, houses and walls/palisade as the starting assets. Houses also stand outside the defences.

The lord's family is the wealthiest locally and has governed the area for generations. Its military prominence is recent or newly reactivated. It is not a rival kingdom and cannot threaten the New Empire. It governs local affairs under a larger authority.

Its power rests on land, resources, protection and local legitimacy. Minor settlements depend on the lord for protection and access to the wider order; the lord depends on them for food, labour, timber, minerals and other resources. This relationship is unequal but reciprocal, not simply bullying.

A minor settlement building permanent military infrastructure may be understood as a claim to independent authority. The lord might negotiate, demand the building be dismantled, offer a charter, or use force; the response need not always be identical.

## Present moment
The region has enjoyed relative peace for a long time. The established families maintained their occupations and local arrangements, and the lordship needed only a modest garrison or limited fortifications.

Now reports suggest that dangerous magic and forces associated with the Everlasting Court may be growing in influence after a long quiet period. Strange events, disappearances, troubling rumours and unrest are being reported in some places. The pattern is not fully understood, and not every disturbance is necessarily connected to the Court.

The lord's family has strengthened or expanded its military posture in response. The walls, barracks and military presence may have older origins but are being used more seriously now. More soldiers may arrive as the campaign progresses. Whether the lord has concrete evidence, rumours, imperial instructions or a mixture remains open.

## Wider setting
- **New Empire:** younger, centralised and expansionist; values administration, order, law, productivity and material progress; prohibits magic.
- **Old Kingdom:** older, sophisticated civilisation that coexisted with magic for a long time. Its exact relationship with this region is undecided.
- **Old Peoples:** diverse communities with their own customs and traditions; not a single united faction.
- **Everlasting Court:** secretive dangerous magical faction associated with attempts to overcome human limits. It may influence events beyond its strongholds, but not every local problem should be attributed to it.
- **Hidden Society of Traditional Magic:** provisional concept for a secret society preserving older magical knowledge; its organisation and relationship with the Old Kingdom remain open.

## Local society
Several families have lived in the region for generations. Innkeeping, smithing, trading and farming may pass from parent to child over long periods. The innkeeper knows local families personally—their relationships, reputations, debts and disputes.

Residents know that distant cities and more sophisticated life exist, but many rarely travel far. News arrives through soldiers, merchants, travellers and officials. Their culture is grounded and historically textured; do not portray them as primitive or ignorant.

## Worldview and religion
The innkeeper and many locals have a pagan-like, pre-Christian outlook, but the religion, gods, rituals and cosmology are deliberately undefined. For the innkeeper, honour involves fulfilling one's role, maintaining reputation, respecting ancestors and continuing a family tradition well. Offerings to gods may be practical acts of respect intended to maintain favour or good fortune, rather than expressions of guilt or submission. Do not invent named deities or detailed rites yet.

## Scope
The current map is a starting point, not a limit. First develop the existing settlement and buildings. Add terrain, locations and settlements later if useful; do not require new landscape assets to begin developing the inn.

## Design principle
Begin small: make one building and one character convincing within the region before expanding. Let the wider world emerge naturally through conversation, relationships, trade, military activity, rumours and events.
