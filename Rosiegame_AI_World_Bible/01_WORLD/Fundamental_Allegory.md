# The Fundamental Allegory: Magic, Civilisation, and the New Revelation

**Status:** FOUNDATIONAL WORLDVIEW — core thematic direction established; individual historical details and terminology may remain provisional.

## Purpose of the Allegory

Magic is the world's concrete analogue for the human spirit: imagination, personality, desire, ambition, creativity, cruelty and the capacity to transform the world.

The story uses magic to make a moral and spiritual problem visible. In the player's own world, people can readily understand that magic is not inherently good or evil; what matters is how it is used and the relationship people have with it. In this fictional world, however, most civilised people do not begin from that understanding. They treat magic as something that must be classified: good or evil, permitted or forbidden, belonging to us or belonging to the enemy.

The revolutionary idea is not simply that some magic is good and some magic is bad. It is that magic itself cannot be understood through the rigid in-group/out-group categories that dominate civilised thought. The question is how human power and spirit should relate to life.

## The Two Great Powers: Different Expressions of the Same Error

The New Empire and the opposing magical power appear to be enemies, but at a deeper level they express the same underlying error: the attempt to impose an abstract ideal upon life and overcome its limits.

- **The New Empire:** represents excessive order, centralisation, uniformity and control. It believes human problems can be solved by organising and disciplining life. Its system can become totalitarian, treating people as components of the state.
- **The Everlasting Court / opposing magical power:** represents the pursuit of power without limits, the attempt to overcome natural constraints and perfect existence through magic. It can become destructive and exploitative, treating living beings as resources.

Their methods differ, but both detach human power from a healthy relationship with the living world. They are not moral opposites in the deepest sense; they are related failures of civilisation.

The story's real moral contrast is not Empire versus Court. It is the worldview embodied by both of them versus the small group who discover a different way.

## The New Revelation

A figure modelled on the role of Jesus has just appeared in the world's present. He challenges the dominant assumption that magic must be inherently good or evil according to which group possesses it.

His teaching offers a new moral and spiritual framework: power and the human spirit must be understood and directed through a proper relationship with life, rather than through group allegiance, domination or abstract systems. Magic is not to be simply abolished, nor pursued without limit.

He knowingly sacrifices himself. The sacrifice is central to his message, but the exact historical circumstances and the details of what he intended his followers to understand remain open for development.

His followers form a small, persecuted movement. They are the only group that consciously understands the new principle and can deliberately combine knowledge of magic with the moral direction needed to use it rightly. They are the most relatable people by the intended thematic standard, although individual followers can still be flawed, uncertain or inconsistent.

This movement is the origin or core of the secret magical society previously discussed. Its exact institutional history can be developed separately.

## The Tribal Peoples and the State Before Civilisation

The tribal peoples are not simply a third political faction that has consciously synthesised the Empire and the Court. They inhabit a different state of understanding.

They do not necessarily ask whether magic is inherently good or evil. Magic is part of the world they live in, like human nature itself. Their knowledge is embedded in custom, ancestry, experience, relationships and practice, rather than a written theory about magic.

They are an analogy for a more original state of harmony with nature — an Eden-like condition in which human life has not yet been separated from the natural world through the abstractions and ambitions of advanced civilisation.

They are not the same as the Christian movement. They preserve a kind of unselfconscious harmony, while the new revelation offers a conscious moral understanding of how power should be used after civilisation has developed and its dangers have become apparent.

Their way of life is vulnerable to the immense power of the civilised kingdoms. Their decline may be inevitable as civilisation expands, even though they preserve something valuable that the kingdoms have lost. They should feel culturally and psychologically different from the civilised characters, not like modern people wearing tribal clothing.

## The Moral Structure

The intended moral structure is deliberately not a simple balance of equally valid philosophies.

- The Empire and the Everlasting Court are both expressions of civilisation's dangerous separation from the living world.
- The tribal peoples retain an older, largely untheorised relationship with nature.
- The new movement following the Christ-like figure brings the conscious moral revelation needed to guide the human spirit and the power of civilisation.

The underlying moral truth is clear within the world's intended cosmology, even though characters can misunderstand it, reject it, doubt what they have witnessed, or behave inconsistently with what they believe.

Moral complexity should come from limited knowledge, human weakness, competing loyalties, historical misunderstanding and the consequences of action. It should not erase the fundamental distinction between the central error and the new revelation.

## Characterisation and Dialogue Implications

Most civilised NPCs should instinctively frame magic in categorical terms: good or evil, ours or theirs, lawful or forbidden. They may disagree strongly about which category applies, but they do not naturally begin with the idea that magic is a morally neutral capacity whose meaning depends on the relationship and purpose guiding its use.

The new movement's insight should feel genuinely revolutionary and strange in this setting. Characters outside it may misunderstand it, dismiss it, fear it or regard it as dangerous.

The tribal peoples should not deliver philosophical lectures about balance. Their outlook should appear through habits, practices, customs, relationships and reactions. They may not understand why civilised people insist on abstracting magic into a universal moral category.

The Christ-like figure and his followers should not simply be another faction claiming that its own magic is good. Their difference is the moral and spiritual framework through which they understand the human spirit and its power.

The New Empire and the Everlasting Court can have different institutions, aesthetics and methods while still expressing the same underlying error. Their conflict should not imply that one is the true moral alternative to the other.

## Tone and Intended Player Experience

The world should not make every faction equally relatable. The civilised powers may be frightening, alienating or difficult to sympathise with because their systems subordinate living people to abstractions. The tribal peoples should feel different from them, not merely like a kinder version of civilisation. The small movement following the new revelation should be the primary point of moral and emotional recognition for the player.

The player should be able to recognise the allegory without every character spelling it out. Magic externalises the danger of human power detached from its proper moral context. The two great powers show different outcomes of that separation. The tribal peoples preserve a fading relationship with nature. The new revelation arrives at the moment when the civilisation's power makes the underlying problem urgent.

## Open Questions

The following details are not fixed by this foundational statement:
- The Christ-like figure's name, precise biography, teachings and circumstances of sacrifice.
- The exact relationship between his followers and the existing secret magical society.
- The names and historical sequence of the two great powers and the War of the Two Crowns.
- How the new movement's ideas spread, and how different NPCs first encounter them.
- How much of the underlying truth is objectively demonstrable in-world, versus understood through faith, experience and interpretation.
- The precise cultural identities, customs and histories of individual tribal peoples.

Do not use this file to invent answers to those open questions without further direction.
