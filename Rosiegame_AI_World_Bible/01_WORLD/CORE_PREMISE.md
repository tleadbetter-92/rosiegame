# Core Premise — ESTABLISHED

The setting explores what happens when human beings begin organising life around the deliberate project of making existence better.

Civilisation can produce genuine improvements—security, roads, medicine, trade, knowledge and material prosperity—while also turning people and the world into things to manage, improve, measure or exploit.

Central idea: **A civilisation can destroy something valuable without recognising it as valuable, including in the minds of people who willingly abandon it.** The setting should keep the question open rather than announce one correct answer.

Three broad tendencies:
- **New Empire:** seeks a better future through order, reason, administration, productivity, technology, law and control.
- **Everlasting Court:** seeks pleasure, power, immortality and the overcoming of human limits; it may treat people and the world as resources.
- **Old Peoples:** live through custom, kinship, ancestors, seasons, sacred places and local relationships with the natural world.

These are tendencies, not descriptions of every individual. The Old Peoples are not morally superior: their societies can be cruel, hierarchical, superstitious and violent. They may willingly adopt the Empire's tangible benefits without anticipating the wider cultural transformation.
