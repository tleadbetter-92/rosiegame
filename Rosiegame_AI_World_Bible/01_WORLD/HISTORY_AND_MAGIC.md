# History and Magic — CORE OUTLINE ESTABLISHED

An older civilisation contained a royal faction and scholars who pursued overcoming human limits through magic. Their promises included ending disease, hunger, ageing, death and suffering. Their magic genuinely worked in some respects and attracted nobles, scholars, merchants and ordinary people.

Over time, the pursuit became extractive: experiments, taking vitality, treating people as subjects or resources, preservation and immortality, and political coercion. This contributed to the **War of the Two Crowns**. Both sides committed atrocities; cities were destroyed, populations massacred and magical horrors created. Neither side was morally spotless.

The anti-Court faction won and founded the New Empire, or its predecessors. The victors banned magic, destroyed texts and persecuted practitioners. They concluded that magic itself was the problem rather than the deeper impulse to control and perfect existence. The Empire now pursues order and progress through non-magical means and may have misunderstood the war's lesson.

Magic is prohibited in the New Empire, but law, enforcement and public belief differ. Some people fear it, some are curious or sympathetic, some ignore it, and some seek it when desperate. Rural customs and folklore survive. Attitudes vary by region, class, experience and official zeal.

The historical parallel to the displacement of older religious/cultural ways is broad, not a simplistic claim that one real-world religion is solely responsible for civilisation's changes.
