# Quest Categories — TWO TYPES ESTABLISHED; LABELS/COLOURS OPEN

## 1. Military, diplomatic, political and economic quests
These concern the ordinary business of society. They can be pragmatic and morally ambiguous. Examples: defend a settlement, raid territory, assassinate a commander, destroy supplies, negotiate an alliance, settle a border dispute, persuade a lord to change sides, expose a rival, suppress a rebellion, secure a trade route.

The player may act for a ruler, village, faction, merchant or personal interest. Success and failure can change territory, resources, relationships and political/military state. These quests do not need to deliver a moral lesson.

## 2. “Coloured quests”
The term is a working label; final name and UI colours are OPEN. These arise from unusual encounters, mysteries, local legends, supernatural events, personal stories and experiences outside ordinary political objectives.

They may be funny, unsettling, tragic, beautiful or inexplicable. They should not all end in combat or receive a clear explanation. They may reward curiosity or change the player's understanding of the world rather than help a faction win.

Examples only: a village where nobody dreams; a woman whose returned son seems not to be her son; a ruin that appears different to everyone; a stranger offering to remove the player's worst memory but refusing money; letters arriving from a supposedly dead king.

These should not merely be ordinary quests with magic added. The ordinary system can prioritise objectives and consequences; coloured quests can prioritise atmosphere, discovery, character and uncertainty.
