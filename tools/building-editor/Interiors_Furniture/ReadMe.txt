WHAT'S IN THIS ZIP

  FBX/       one .fbx per model, for Unity and Unreal.

  Contents.csv  every item: name, group, size, triangles.

HOW THE MODELS ARE MADE
- 1 unit = 1 metre. A person is 1.8 m tall.
- Every model uses one material and one 256 x 256 palette texture, the same one
  across the whole library, so they all share a single material in your game.
- Flat shaded: each face is one flat colour. Keep it that way on import.
- Each model's origin is where you place it: on the ground for most, at a grid
  corner for pieces that snap together, at the hand grip for tools and weapons,
  and where it fixes to a wall for things that hang.

IMPORTING
Godot 4: drag the glTF folder in. The texture comes with each model.
Unity: drag the FBX folder and Textures in. Make one material with
  mdvl_palette.png as its base map and give it to the models. On the texture,
  set Filter Mode to Point and Compression to None. On the models, set Normals
  to Calculate and Smoothing Angle to 0.
Unreal 5: import the FBX files and the texture. Make one material with the
  texture into Base Color, Roughness about 0.85, and set the texture's filter
  to Nearest.
On any engine: nearest (no smoothing) filtering keeps the colours crisp.

FURNISHING A ROOM
Everything is sized for a 2.4 m room with a 2.05 m doorway, the rooms the
Village Builder pack builds. Nothing standing is taller than 1.95 m.
- Heights: a seat is 45 cm, a table top 76 cm, a work top 90 cm, a
  mattress 50 cm. Any chair goes at any table.
- At no turn, a piece faces -y: put it with its back to a north wall and
  it faces into the room. A bed's head is at its -x end.
- Things that hang (paintings, tapestries, shelves, sconces, the torch,
  the mirror, the clock) have their origin on the wall face and hang into
  -y. The chandelier's origin is on the ceiling.

CHESTS, LIGHTS AND FLAMES
- Each chest is the box with its lid off. Put its lid this far from the
  chest's origin, turned the same way, and it is shut:
    chest_01 + chest_lid_01   (0, 0.23, 0.34)
    chest_02 + chest_lid_02   (0, 0.31, 0.46)
    chest_03 + chest_lid_03   (0, 0.21, 0.32)
  The lid's origin is its hinge: turn it about its own x by a negative
  angle to open it (-110 stands it open).
- The candle, candelabra, oil lamp, wall torch and brazier come lit. Scale
  the bone named fire to zero to put one out: the flame shrinks to nothing
  and the rest of the model stays where it is.
- The chandelier and sconce come unlit; flame_01 is a candle flame for
  them. It goes on the top of the candle. From each one's origin:
    chandelier_01   six, 0.26 m out at every 60 degrees from +x, z -0.285
    sconce_01       (0, -0.09, 0.155)
- The lantern comes unlit too, with closed horn panels and no candle in it.
  Light it with a point light inside at (0, 0, 0.10), shadows off.
- flame_02 is a bigger flame: for the forge at (-0.36, 0.23, 0.57), the
  stove at (0, -0.29, 0.166), or a fireplace.
- Lights carry no light of their own: put a point light by each flame.

THE KITCHEN
- The cauldron hangs from the pot crane's hook: put it unturned at
  (0, -0.35, 0.15) from the crane's origin. Or stand it in a fire.

MODELS THAT MOVE
These models carry bones and an animation that loops: flames that flicker,
some with sparks rising from them. Each is still one mesh with one material.
Godot 4: use the glTF files. The animation's name ends in "loop", so
  Godot imports it looping; play it from the model's AnimationPlayer.
Unity: in the FBX import settings set Rig to Generic, and on the clip
  tick Loop Time.
Unreal 5: import the skeleton and the animation with the FBX, and set the
  animation to loop where you play it.

mdvl_interiors_candle_01
  Bones: root, fire, flame_0 with its _tip. Animation: burn_loop.
mdvl_interiors_flame_01
  Bones: root, flame_0 with its _tip. Animation: burn_loop.
mdvl_interiors_flame_02
  Bones: root, flame_0 to flame_4 (each with a _tip), spark_0 to spark_1.
  Animation: burn_loop.
mdvl_interiors_walltorch_01
  Bones: root, fire, flame_0 to flame_4 (each with a _tip), spark_0 to
  spark_1. Animation: burn_loop.

HOW THESE WERE MADE
Every model here was built by Python scripts in Blender, written with the help of an AI coding assistant. 4 of the 25 models started as 3D meshes generated by Tripo AI from AI-made concept art; the scripts then reshaped, cleaned, repainted and reduced them.
